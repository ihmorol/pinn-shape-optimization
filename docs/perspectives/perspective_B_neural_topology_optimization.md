Scope: how NN-parameterized topology optimization (TO) represents shape,
embeds elasticity physics, and optimizes — and what transfers to
physics-informed shape optimization of small mobile robot bodies.

## (a) Synthesis — 5 key lessons from neural TO for shape optimization with geometric control points

1. **The parameterization is a regularizer.** Hoyer et al. showed that wrapping a density field in an MLP and doing plain gradient descent on network weights outperforms direct pixel optimization because the network's spectral bias acts as a free, adaptive filter — smoother, more connected structures without hand-tuned convolution filters. For control-point shape optimization, the analog is: a smooth, low-dimensional spline/SDF basis *is* the filter; fewer, well-conditioned control points can beat dense voxel fields on final compliance and manufacturability.
2. **Keep the PDE solver in the loop and differentiate through it.** Every high-performing method in this field (Hoyer, NTopo, TOM, JAX-FEM-based, X-CAD, ToRoS) computes elasticity physics with a real FEA solve and backpropagates through assembly and solve (autodiff replaces hand-derived adjoints). PINN-based solvers (CPINN-TO) remove the mesh entirely but remain slower and less accurate for elasticity — they are a niche, not the mainstream. Lesson: for robot bodies, use a differentiable FEM solve, not a learned/PINN physics surrogate, wherever possible.
3. **Length-scale and solidity constraints must be re-imposed explicitly.** Neural reparameterization eliminates voxel checkerboards but introduces feature-collapse and gray-region artifacts; every mature method adds a projected-density chain (filter → Heaviside/projection → SIMP exponent) or an explicit level-set/Heaviside mapping to get crisp black-and-white designs. Control-point methods need the equivalent: minimum-feature-size penalties or curvature bounds on the spline/SDF.
4. **Conditioning the network on a latent code converts TO from "one design" to "design space."** Modulated neural fields (TOM) and the OpenTO foundation model (latent autoencoder + diffusion prior) generate many diverse, near-optimal structures from one run or one pretrained model, conditional on loads/constraints. This is directly relevant for robot bodies where several near-optimal shell topologies should be explored before committing to one for integration.
5. **Co-optimizing CAD parameters through non-conforming physics is the most direct precedent.** X-CAD optimizes B-spline control points of a CAD model through an extended-FEM solve on a fixed background mesh (no remeshing); ToRoS optimizes reinforcement placement on robotic skins; NeuralTOMO co-optimizes topology, manufacturable layer curves, and fiber paths. These demonstrate that geometric (CAD) parameters and physics can share one differentiable loss even when geometry and mesh do not conform.

## (c) Transferable lessons for robot-body shape optimization

1. **What parameterizations work.** Three families dominate, in increasing freedom: (i) geometric/CAD parameters — B-spline control points (X-CAD) — best when the body must remain manufacturable and CAD-editable, with gradients reaching them cleanly through non-conforming background solvers; (ii) neural implicit fields (SDF/level-set MLPs, NTopo, Daviet et al.) — best when topology itself should change; (iii) voxel/density fields behind NN reparameterization (Hoyer, TOuNN) — the research baseline, least useful when a clean CAD boundary is the deliverable. A hybrid — spline/SDF boundary + background differentiable physics — is the state-of-the-art recipe for bodies that must keep sensor mounts, wheel wells, and skin continuity.
2. **What physics resolution is needed.** Linear elasticity with SIMP/Heaviside-projected stiffness is sufficient for stiffness- and mass-driven shell design; stress constraints (wheel mounts, leg joints) need far finer discretization and remain open. Small-robot bodies live in the linear regime.
3. **Gradient quality issues.** Autodiff through the physics gives exact adjoint gradients, but composition with an NN parameterization changes the landscape: Sanu et al. show architecture and initialization reshape the optima reached; annealing and energy formulations exist to keep gradients informative. Practical transfers: Adam with lr decay rather than MMA when the design is network-parameterized; expect chaotic sensitivity near topological events.
4. **Constraints need re-imposition, not luck.** Volume, minimum feature size, symmetry, motor-mount keep-outs are handled as differentiable penalties, projection operators, or conditioning inputs. For robot bodies, encode actuator mounts, battery boxes, and sealing as fixed/void masks fed to both the generator and the physics.
5. **Multi-objective handling.** Two working patterns: (i) weighted/penalized composite losses tuned with adaptive penalty weights (TOuNN, NeuralTOMO); (ii) generate-then-select — a conditioned generative model produces a diverse near-optimal set and the task-level objective picks among candidates, with gradient-based polishing. Pattern (ii) avoids brittle scalarization when objectives conflict.
6. **Exploit the solver-in-the-loop anchor, distrust physics-free surrogates.** Methods whose only physics is baked into training data generalize impressively but still need FEM verification; the reviews (Woldseth, Sanu) both warn against replacing the state solver with an NN. Differentiable physics must sit inside the loop as the source of truth.
7. **Co-design beats sequential design.** DiffAqua and ToRoS demonstrate that shape + control (+ material, + fabrication path) optimized jointly in one differentiable loop outperforms optimizing the body then the controller.

## (d) Repeated loss/constraint formulations

**1. Canonical density-based TO (underlies #1–#4, #9, #11, #14):**

$$\min_{\boldsymbol{\rho}} \; c(\boldsymbol{\rho}) = \mathbf{U}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{K}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\,\mathbf{U} = \sum_{e} (\rho_e)^{p}\, \mathbf{u}_e^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{k}_0\, \mathbf{u}_e$$
$$\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbf{K}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\,\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{F}, \qquad V(\boldsymbol{\rho}) = \sum_e \rho_e v_e \le V^{*}, \qquad 0 < \rho_{\min} \le \rho_e \le 1$$

with SIMP-projected stiffness $E_e = E_{\min} + \rho_e^{p}(E_0 - E_{\min})$, penalty $p \approx 3$.

**2. Neural reparameterization chain (Hoyer #1; TOuNN #3; NTopo #4):** network → filter → projection → physical density:

$$\bar{\rho}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\tanh\!\big(\beta\,\eta\big) + \tanh\!\big(\beta(\tilde{\rho} - \eta)\big)}{\tanh(\beta\eta) + \tanh\!\big(\beta(1-\eta)\big)}, \qquad \min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \; \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = c\big(\bar{\rho}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\big) + \lambda_V \left[\max\!\Big(0, \tfrac{V(\bar{\rho}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{V^{*}} - 1\Big)\right]^{2} + \lambda_s \int \|\nabla \bar{\rho}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|^2\, d\mathbf{x}$$

**3. Physics-in-the-loop gradient (all FEM-in-the-loop methods):** state solve then autodiff/adjoint:

$$\mathbf{K}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\,\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{F}, \qquad \frac{\partial c}{\partial \boldsymbol{\rho}} = -\,\mathbf{U}^{\mathsf T} \frac{\partial \mathbf{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\rho}}\, \mathbf{U}$$

**4. PINN-embedded elasticity (CPINN-TO #9):**

$$\mathcal{L} = \lambda_r \sum_i \big\| \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_u}; \rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_\rho}) + \mathbf{b} \big\|^2_{\mathbf{x}_i} + \lambda_b \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\Gamma} \big\| \mathbf{u}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_u} - \bar{\mathbf{u}} \big\|^2 + \lambda_c\, c\big(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_\rho}\big) + \lambda_V \left[\max(0, V/V^{*}-1)\right]^2$$

**5. CAD control-point parameterization (X-CAD #5):** B-spline boundary with control points $\mathbf{P}_k$ as design variables, evaluated on a fixed background mesh via XFEM/cut-cells:

$$\mathbf{S}(\boldsymbol{\xi}) = \sum_k N_k(\boldsymbol{\xi})\, \mathbf{P}_k, \qquad \min_{\{\mathbf{P}_k\}} c\big(\mathbf{K}(\{\mathbf{P}_k\})\big) \;\; \text{s.t.} \;\; V(\{\mathbf{P}_k\}) \le V^{*},\; \|\ddot{\mathbf{S}}\| \le \kappa_{\max}$$

**6. Level-set / SDF neural form (NTopo #4, Daviet #13):**

$$\rho(\mathbf{x}) = H_\epsilon\big(\phi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\big), \qquad H_\epsilon(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}\Big(1 + \tfrac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\big(\tfrac{z}{\epsilon}\big)\Big)$$

**7. Diversity/generative objectives (TOM #15, OpenTO #16):**

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}}\Big[ c\big(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot;\mathbf{z})\big) + \lambda_V V(\mathbf{z}) \Big] - \lambda_d\, \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z} \neq \mathbf{z}'} \big[ d\big(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot;\mathbf{z}), \rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\cdot;\mathbf{z}')\big) \big]$$
