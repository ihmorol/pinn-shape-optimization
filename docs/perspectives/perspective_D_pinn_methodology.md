## (a) Synthesis: five failure modes and the established mitigations

1. **Competing soft-constraint losses are stiff and unbalanced — fix with adaptive loss weighting.** PINN training couples PDE-residual and boundary/initial-condition terms whose gradient magnitudes and convergence rates differ wildly; plain Adam converges to residual-dominated or BC-dominated dead ends. Backing: learning-rate annealing from gradient statistics (**Wang, Teng & Perdikaris 2021**, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 43(5):B1105–B1132) and NTK-eigenvalue-based weighting (**Wang, Yu & Perdikaris 2022**, J. Comput. Phys. 449:110768); pointwise self-adaptive weights (**McClenny & Braga-Neto 2023**, JCP 474:111738). *For a shape-optimization loop: adaptive weighting is not an option, it is the default; a fixed-λ PINN inside an optimizer will silently trade BC accuracy against residual accuracy as the shape changes.*
2. **Spectral bias of MLPs kills high-frequency and multi-scale fields — fix with Fourier features (or plane-wave activations) for acoustics.** Standard MLPs learn low frequencies first (**Rahaman et al. 2019**, ICML, PMLR 97:5301–5310); Fourier feature input mappings restore high-frequency capacity (**Tancik et al. 2020**, NeurIPS, arXiv:2006.10739); in PINNs the NTK eigenvector bias of Fourier features must be matched to the wavenumber, with multi-scale variants for multi-k problems (**Wang, Wang & Perdikaris 2021**, CMAME 387:114166). *Directly relevant: a robot's microphone/speaker band (speech ≈ 0.1–8 kHz in a body of decimeter scale) means k·D of O(1–100); naive tanh PINNs will under-resolve exactly the modes that matter.*
3. **PINNs can fail on embarrassingly simple problems; training order, causality, and collocation coverage are the levers.** Krishnapriyan et al. showed failure even on 1-D convection and wave equations and proposed curriculum + sequence-to-sequence formulations (**Krishnapriyan et al. 2021**, NeurIPS, arXiv:2109.01050); causal (time-marching) loss re-weighting fixes advection-dominated time-dependent cases (**Wang, Sankaran & Perdikaris 2024**, CMAME 421:116813); residual-based adaptive refinement (RAR, introduced in **Lu et al. 2021**, SIAM Review 63(1):208–228 / DeepXDE) and its systematic comparison (**Wu, Zhu, Tan, Kartha & Lu 2023**, CMAME 403(Part A):115671, which found grid/uniform sampling is the best baseline for smooth problems and RAR variants help near sources/singularities) control where collocation points go. *For our use: robots have steady thermal fields with hotspots (chips, motor drivers) and acoustic fields with sources (speaker port) and edges — sample adaptively there or accept silent error.*
4. **Boundary conditions are where PINNs quietly lie.** Missing or under-weighted BCs produce plausible-but-wrong fields: Wu et al. showed Helmholtz PINNs without absorbing/PML boundary losses give inaccurate wavefields even in smooth media (**Wu, Hossein-Nasab, Operto & Ma 2023**, Geophysics 88(4):T185–T198); Veerababu & Ghosh found vanishing-gradient failure of frequency-domain acoustic PINNs at higher frequencies and advocate **building the BCs into the network ansatz** (**Veerababu & Ghosh 2024**, J. Sound Vib. 585:118476); hard-constraint (augmented-Lagrangian) enforcement beats soft penalties for inverse design (**Lu et al. 2021 — hPINN**, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 43(6):B1105–B1132). *Our robot bodies have Robin (convection + radiation) thermal BCs and Neumann/impedance acoustic BCs — these must be hard-enforced or adaptively weighted, never one soft term among five.*
5. **Be honest about what PINNs buy you in an optimization loop.** Large-scale benchmarks (20+ PDEs, dozens of variants) conclude that PINNs still trail classical solvers on most forward problems and that no single variant dominates (**Hao et al. 2024 — PINNacle**, NeurIPS D&B, arXiv:2306.08827; **Takamoto et al. 2022 — PDEBench**, NeurIPS D&B, arXiv:2210.07182). The pragmatic pattern that emerges from application papers: use the PINN as a *meshless, differentiable, adjoint-free forward map inside the design loop* (autodiff gives d(output)/d(shape) for free), preferably pretrained/updated across designs rather than retrained from scratch — see hPINN photonic/flow inverse design, PINN-replaces-FEA topology optimization (PINNTO, Eng. Struct. 278:115484, 2023), and SIMP-with-pretrained-PINN thermal TO (**Jahanbakhsh et al. 2026**, IJHMT 255(Part 2):127902). For robot-body shape optimization, that is exactly the defensible niche: no remeshing per design iteration, trivially differentiable gradients — at the cost of per-instance accuracy that must be certified against a classical solver.

## (c) Practical recipe (small robot body: warm electronics + speaker + microphone)

### C.1 Steady heat conduction with Robin (convection) and radiation BCs

Nondimensionalize first: θ = (T − T∞)/(T_h − T∞), x = x̃/L, with Biot Bi = hL/k and radiation number Nr = εσT_ref³L/k.

$$r_{\rm pde} = \nabla\cdot\big(\tilde k(\theta)\,\nabla\theta\big) + \tilde q(\mathbf{x}) = 0, \qquad r_{\rm conv} = \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial n} + Bi\,\theta \Big|_{\Gamma_{\rm conv}}$$

(with radiation linearized as $h_{rad} = 4\varepsilon\sigma T_\infty^3$ and folded into $Bi$ when ΔT is small).

- **Architecture:** 4–6 hidden layers × 64–200 tanh units (thermal fields are smooth; spectral bias is not the binding constraint).
- **BC handling:** hard-enforce Dirichlet by ansatz; keep Robin/radiation as adaptively weighted soft terms.
- **Weights:** learning-rate annealing (Wang–Teng–Perdikaris) or NTK balancing; expect $w_{bc} \gg w_f$ initially.

### C.2 Helmholtz acoustics with Neumann / impedance BCs

Represent the complex field with two real outputs p = u + iv; scattered-field formulation so the network learns an O(1) quantity:

$$r_{u} = \nabla^2 u + k^2 u - \mathrm{Re}\,S, \qquad r_{\rm N} = \partial_n p|_{\Gamma_{rigid}} = 0, \qquad r_{\rm Z} = \partial_n p - ikp/Z_c|_{\Gamma_{port}} = 0$$

plus a PML/absorbing loss on truncated open-air boundaries (Wu et al. 2023).

- **Architecture:** mandatory Fourier features with σ ∼ k, or multi-scale Fourier features across the design band; 4–8 layers × 100–256 units; two-output complex representation.
- **Sampling:** dense uniform grid baseline (best for smooth fields per Wu et al. 2023); RAR refresh of 5–10% of points every ~1k iterations near the speaker port, edges, and field maxima.

### C.3 Optimizer, stopping, and the shape-optimization loop

- Adam (lr 1e-3 → 1e-4, cosine decay) then full-batch L-BFGS; keep a held-out residual as validation.
- **Warm-start each new shape from the previous shape's weights** (transfer learning) and stop early on plateau — do not retrain from scratch.
- **Certify every candidate design with a classical solve before accepting it** (PINNacle/PDEBench evidence).

## (d) Gaps — PINN-based thermal/acoustic shape optimization for robots

1. **Robin/radiation BCs are essentially absent from thermal shape-optimization loops** — demonstrated only in forward PINN solves, not inside an optimizer, and never shape-dependent.
2. **Acoustic shape optimization with PINNs stops at low and single frequency** — the speech-band sweep a talking robot needs would require per-frequency networks, multi-scale Fourier features, and PML losses jointly; none of the verified works couple a Helmholtz PINN to enclosure-geometry optimization.
3. **No benchmark measures optimization-loop quality** — design-trajectory quality, gradient consistency ∂J/∂φ vs classical adjoints, or warm-start vs retrain cost-accuracy frontiers are unmeasured; residual-loss "surrogate overfitting" (designs that satisfy the PINN, not the physics) is a known-in-principle but unquantified risk.
4. **Thermo-acoustic coupling and robot-data assimilation are unexplored** — electronics heat → sound speed variations → microphone response; on-board thermistors/mic arrays as PINN data sources; closed-loop data-assimilating PINN shape optimization on real hardware has no reference implementation.
