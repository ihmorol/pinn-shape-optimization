Scope: how mainstream works use PINNs / neural PDE solvers for shape and
design optimization, and how the shape parameterization is coupled to the
network and to gradients.

## (a) Synthesis — what the mainstream does and how physics enters the optimization loop

- **Two architectures dominate.** (1) *Two-stage surrogate + optimize*: a parametric PINN is trained once against the PDE residual (plus sparse data), then shape/design parameters are optimized by backpropagating gradients through the trained network — no mesh adjoint is ever built (Sun et al. 2023; Wong et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024). (2) *One-stage co-training*: design variables and field solution are updated in a single differentiable loss combining residual/energy terms with the design objective (hPINN of Lu et al. 2021; TOuNN's neural density field whose compliance is evaluated by a differentiable FEM pass).
- **Physics enters the loop as (i) a PDE-residual loss** — autodiff evaluates $\mathcal{N}[u_\theta]$ at collocation points so first/second derivatives w.r.t. inputs and weights are free, making shape sensitivities $\partial J/\partial \mathbf{z}$ a by-product of automatic differentiation through the network — or as **(ii) a variational/energy form** (Deep Ritz minimizes the PDE energy; hPINN and TOuNN minimize compliance), which is the preferred route for structural/design problems because energy integrals are better conditioned than pointwise residuals.
- **Shape-to-network coupling is the real design choice**, and four mainstream tricks recur: (a) shape parameters $\mathbf{z}$ (CST/Bezier/Hicks-Henne) fed as extra network inputs to make the PINN parametric in geometry; (b) a *reference-to-physical domain mapping* (mesh transformation) so the network always sees a fixed box while the true airfoil deforms (Wassing et al. 2024); (c) *implicit geometry conditioning* via signed distance functions or DeepSDF latent codes (Park et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2024; Kraus 2024); (d) *geometry-aware input encodings* — Fourier features (Tancik et al. 2020) against spectral bias, Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions for complex shapes (Δ-PINNs).
- **Hard constraints beat soft penalties inside optimization loops.** Exact boundary enforcement via distance functions ($u_\theta = g + D\cdot\mathcal{N}$, hPINN; Aygun et al. 2023) removes the loss-balancing pathology that otherwise stalls gradient-based design iterations, and domain decomposition (cPINN/XPINN) extends PINNs to complex, multi-part geometries relevant to real bodies.
- **The evidence base is single-physics, mostly 2D, and airfoil-dominated.** External aerodynamics (lift/drag) and heat-conduction topology optimization account for most mainstream shape-optimization studies; thermal-device surrogates (power modules) are emerging in 2025; *no mainstream work yet does coupled multi-physics (aero–thermal–structural–acoustic) shape optimization of a robot-like body with PINNs* — confirmed by the 2021–2026 surveys (Karniadakis et al. 2021; Michaloglou et al. 2025), which flag inverse/design problems as PINNs' strongest use case while noting the application gap in coupled embodied systems.

## (c) Gaps and opportunities for PINN-based shape optimization of small mobile robot bodies

- **No coupled multi-physics design loop exists.** Mainstream demonstrations are single-physics (external aero; heat conduction; device thermals). A robot hull simultaneously trades drag (aero), battery/actuator temperature (thermal), shell stiffness and tip-over stability (structural), and radiated noise (acoustics). hPINN-style energy losses plus XPINN-style domain decomposition (one subnetwork per body panel/module) are the natural mainstream tools to fuse these — nobody has assembled them into one robot-body optimizer; this is the survey's clearest open niche.
- **Shape parameterizations are built for smooth single surfaces, not assembled machines.** Airfoils use CST/Bezier; even the SDF works encode single watertight objects. Robot bodies are piecewise composites (chassis + wheels/legs + sensor pods + battery bays) whose boundary conditions *change with configuration and gait*. DeepSDF latent codes + Δ-PINN geometry encodings could condition one network across a morphology family, but parameterizing assemblies with moving internal components (and gait-dependent BCs) inside a PINN loss is unsolved.
- **Thermal co-design is the most transferable thread but stops at static devices.** The 2025 IEEE TPEL parameterized-PINN power-module work is exactly the pattern a robot needs (fast transient thermal fields over a duty cycle, moving heat sources: motors, ESCs, battery). Extending it to moving/wheel-mounted heat sources, electro-thermal coupling (copper losses depend on temperature), and packaging inside a hull is open.
- **Acoustics is nearly untouched.** PINN inverse design has been demonstrated in acoustics (hard-constraint PINNs, VU Amsterdam line) and time-domain acoustics is PINN-tractable, but "quiet-by-design" robot bodies — motor-whine/fan-noise shaping via shell geometry coupled to vibration — has no PINN entry; a differentiable acoustic residual on a GINN-generated hull would be novel.
- **Gradients, constraints, and benchmarks.** Mainstream works optimize either through a residual-trained surrogate (fast, but surrogate error can mislead the design gradient) or by co-training with the objective in the loss (accurate, but per-shape cost). For robot bodies the right split — hPINN surrogate for thermal/aero + differentiable FE for stability/manufacturability (TOuNN/GINN style) — is untested; and there is no robot-body analogue of the airfoil benchmarks/datasets (e.g., an SDF+field dataset of robot hulls) that the airfoil community uses to compare methods, which blocks reproducible progress.

## (d) Recurring equations / loss formulations

**1. PINN residual loss (Raissi et al. 2019) — the workhorse:**

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\underbrace{\frac{1}{N_u}\sum_{i=1}^{N_u}\lvert u_\theta(t_i^u,x_i^u)-u_i\rvert^2}_{\text{data}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{N_b}\sum_{i=1}^{N_b}\lvert u_\theta(t_i^b,x_i^b)-g_i\rvert^2}_{\text{BC/IC}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{N_f}\sum_{i=1}^{N_f}\lvert \mathcal{N}[u_\theta](t_i^f,x_i^f)\rvert^2}_{\text{PDE residual, via autodiff}}$$

**2. PDE-constrained shape optimization** (what every shape-optimization paper formalizes; $f(\cdot;\mathbf{z})$ maps design variables to the domain):

$$\min_{\mathbf{z}}\; J\big(u_\theta(\cdot;\mathbf{z}),\,\mathbf{z}\big)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathcal{N}[u_\theta;\mathbf{z}]=0 \;\text{ on } \Omega(\mathbf{z}), \qquad \mathbf{z} \leftarrow \mathbf{z}-\eta\,\frac{\partial J}{\partial \mathbf{z}} \;\text{ (autodiff through the network — no discrete adjoint)}$$

**3. Hard constraint via distance function (hPINN; Aygun et al. — exact BCs, no penalty):**

$$u_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = g(\mathbf{x}) + D(\mathbf{x})\,\mathcal{N}_\theta(\mathbf{x}), \qquad D(\mathbf{x})\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0 \;\Rightarrow\; u_\theta\big|_{\partial\Omega}=g \;\text{ exactly}$$

**4. Variational / energy form (Deep Ritz; hPINN; TOuNN compliance):**

$$\mathcal{E}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\int_\Omega \lVert\nabla u_\theta\rVert^2\,\mathrm{d}x - \int_\Omega f\,u_\theta\,\mathrm{d}x, \qquad \text{(TOuNN: } C(\theta)=\mathbf{F}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{K}(\rho_\theta)^{-1}\mathbf{F},\; \rho_\theta=\sigma(\mathcal{N}_\theta(\mathbf{x}))\text{)}$$

**5. Fourier feature encoding (Tancik et al. 2020) — used in SDF/geometry conditioning:**

$$\gamma(\mathbf{x})=\big[\cos(2\pi \mathbf{B}\mathbf{x}),\,\sin(2\pi \mathbf{B}\mathbf{x})\big]^\mathsf{T}, \quad \mathbf{B}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$$

**6. SDF-conditioned parametric surrogate (Zhang et al.; DeepSDF-style; Kraus) — the shape handle:**

$$\hat{u}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{F}_\phi\!\Big(\mathbf{x},\,\gamma\big(s_{\psi}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})\big),\,\mathbf{z}\Big), \qquad s_{\psi}:\ (\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})\mapsto \text{SDF}_\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}) \;\text{(latent-decoded signed distance)}$$

**7. Simultaneous surrogate + design loss (Sun et al. 2023 pattern):**

$$\min_{\theta,\,\mathbf{z}}\;\; w_r\mathcal{L}_{r}(\theta;\mathbf{z}) + w_b\mathcal{L}_{bc}(\theta;\mathbf{z}) + w_d\mathcal{L}_{data}(\theta) + w_J\, J(\mathbf{z}, u_\theta)$$
